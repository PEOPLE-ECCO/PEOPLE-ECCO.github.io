# Habitat Connectivity: Theoretical Background

## Concept of habitat connectivity

Habitat connectivity describes the degree to which a landscape facilitates or impedes movement between habitat areas. It supports species movement and dispersal, gene flow, nutrient and energy exchange, ecosystem resilience, and biodiversity conservation.

## Structural and functional connectivity

- **Structural connectivity** describes the physical arrangement of habitat patches using spatial characteristics such as distance, size, and adjacency.
- **Functional connectivity** describes how organisms actually move through the landscape and depends on species behaviour and environmental conditions.

The Habitat Connectivity product focuses on structural connectivity derived consistently from spatial data.

## Patch-matrix framework

Connectivity analysis commonly represents the landscape using:

- Habitat patches
- A surrounding non-habitat matrix
- Edges and boundaries
- Corridors, where relevant

## Fragmentation

Habitat fragmentation breaks continuous habitat into smaller, more isolated patches. Typical effects include reduced patch size, increased isolation, stronger edge effects, disrupted species movement, reduced genetic diversity, and increased extinction risk.

## Landscape metrics

Common metrics include:

- Patch size and area
- Patch density and number of patches
- Nearest-neighbour distance
- Edge density and shape complexity
- Aggregation and connectivity indices

Effective mesh size is an aggregation-based metric derived from patch-size distribution. It increases as landscapes become less fragmented.

## Scale and spatial representation

Connectivity is scale-dependent. Raster habitat classifications support large-area patch identification and computation of adjacency- and distance-based indicators, but patterns observed at one resolution or extent may not hold at another.

## Spatial ecology and metapopulations

In metapopulation theory, local populations occupy habitat patches connected by dispersal. Movement between patches supports recolonization following local extinction and contributes to long-term persistence.

## Operational Earth Observation workflow

Satellite-derived habitat maps, patch-based analysis, and landscape metrics can be combined to quantify fragmentation and connectivity at scale, identify conservation priorities, and support spatial planning and restoration.

## Adopted approach

The product converts habitat maps into patch-based representations, computes spatial metrics, and derives connectivity indicators from patch geometry and arrangement. This provides a consistent and scalable balance between ecological theory and operational usability.
